## Context

The Internet is a public space — a **digital Wild West**. Anyone can access it, and new technologies constantly create new criminal opportunities.

---

## Part One: Attackers

### Hacker

- Attacks individuals and websites using a variety of software and malicious programs
- **Contrary to popular belief, most hackers are NOT malicious**
- See themselves as **cyber-altruists and builders**
- Goal: improve networks by finding holes in their security systems
- Once a system is penetrated (**exploit**), the hacker reports the problem to the makers

### Cracker

- **No time for a softly-softly approach**
- Goal: create as much damage as possible, **for profit or just for fun**
- Attacks Internet providers (ISPs) and individuals
- May be angry at an ISP, or attack for the mere thrill of it

> **Key distinction:** Hackers find and report vulnerabilities. Crackers exploit them.

---

## Attack Types

### Smurfing (smurf attack)

One of the most popular cracker attacks against ISPs.

- Cracker **floods the ISP** with so many garbage packets that **all available bandwidth is used up**
- Result: ISP customers **can't send or receive data**, can't use email, browse the web, or use any Internet service

### Virus / Malware

- Takes over people's computers
- **Damages the hard disk**, corrupts or deletes data and files
- Steals **personal information and passwords**

### Ransomware

A form of malicious software (malware):

- Takes over a person's computer
- **Demands a ransom** from the victim
- Promises (not always truthfully) to **restore access to data upon payment**

---

## Part Two: Scams

### Spam

Unsolicited **junk email** sent out by commercial companies, asking you to buy goods and services.

**Typical content:**

- A phone number to call
- An address to send money to
- A website to visit

**How spammers collect addresses:**

- Collect from publicly accessible sources
- Use programs to collect addresses on the web
- Use dictionaries to make **automated guesses** at common usernames at a given domain
- Send solicitations to tens of thousands of people in a single spam mailing

### Phishing

Tech-savvy **con artists and identity thieves**.

**Methods used:**

- Spam emails
- Fake websites
- Phone calls
- Convincing people to download something

**What they target:**

- Bank and credit card account details
- Social security numbers
- Passwords

**How they hide:**

- Attacks are **not sent from the phisher's own computer**
- Instead, a phisher hires a person who controls a **network of thousands of machines** (botnet)

## Key Vocabulary

|Term|Meaning|
|---|---|
|**Lure**|The attractive qualities of something|
|**Exploit**|Penetrating a system's security|
|**Hacker**|Opportunist who finds security holes (mostly non-malicious)|
|**Cracker**|Malicious attacker, pirata informatico|
|**Smurfing**|Flooding ISP bandwidth with garbage packets|
|**Virus**|Malicious program that damages/corrupts a computer|
|**Malware**|Malicious software in general (software maligno)|
|**Ransomware**|Malware that locks data and demands payment ("virus del riscatto")|
|**Spam**|Unsolicited bulk email|
|**Spammer**|Person/company sending spam|
|**Phisher**|Identity thief using tech to extract sensitive info|
|**Con (artist)**|Someone who tricks or cheats|
|**Ransom**|Sum of money demanded for the release of something/someone|
|**Used up**|Having nothing left (bandwidth, resources)|

## Summary: Who Does What

|Actor|Method|Motive|
|---|---|---|
|Hacker|Exploit (find + report)|Improve security|
|Cracker|Exploit (destroy/steal)|Profit / fun / anger|
|Spammer|Mass email|Sell goods/services|
|Phisher|Fake sites, calls, spam|Steal identity / data|
