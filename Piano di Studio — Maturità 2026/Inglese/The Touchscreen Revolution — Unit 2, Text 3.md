## Context

Though keyboards and mice have long been standard, touchscreen phones and tablets offer a **third way** — no tools needed, just fingers. Touchscreens have developed over **50 years** and now deliver a feature-rich environment.

---

## The 3 Main Systems

Of the dozen or so ways to design touchscreens, the three most recent are:

1. **Resistive system**
2. **Capacitive system**
3. **Optical / infrared system**

---

## 1. Resistive System

**How it works:**

- Glass panel covered with a thin **metallic layer** (e.g. indium tin oxide) that conducts electricity
- **Spacers** on the layer support a second metallic layer that resists electricity flow
- Touch (finger or stylus) → two layers make contact → **electrical field changes** → computer calculates coordinates

**Drawback:** Far less accurate than other technologies. Most **don't support multi-touch**.

**Works with:** Finger or stylus (anything that applies pressure)

---

## 2. Capacitive System

**How it works:**

- Glass display **insulated with a conductive layer**
- Touch → some charge transfers to your finger → **capacitive layer charge decreases**
- Circuits at each corner constantly measure the change → computer calculates touch position

**Drawback:** Only registers touches from objects with their own **electromagnetic field** (finger, charged stylus) → **does not work with gloves**

**Works with:** Finger or stylus that picks up electrical charge

---

## 3. Optical / Infrared System

**How it works:**

- **Two optical sensors** placed in opposite corners at the top of the display
- Opposite the sensors: **infrared backlights** positioned to let sensors detect IR light
- Touch → detected as a **shadow** by sensors → signal processor determines where touch occurred

**Advantages over the other systems:**

- Same glass as non-touch screens → **more robust** than treated capacitive models
- **Cheaper to manufacture**
- No light-absorbing layers → **brighter, more accurate display**
- Designed for **multi-touch** (taps, flicks, swipes)
- **Works with gloves** (any object that casts a shadow)
- Lower production cost → increasingly popular

---

## Quick Comparison

|Feature|Resistive|Capacitive|Optical|
|---|---|---|---|
|Accuracy|Low|High|Very high|
|Multi-touch|No|Yes|Yes|
|Works with gloves|Yes|No|Yes|
|Bright display|Reduced|Reduced|Yes (no absorbing layers)|
|Manufacturing cost|Low|Medium|Low|

## Key Vocabulary

|Term|Meaning|
|---|---|
|**Layer**|A quantity or thickness that lies over a surface|
|**Spacer**|A piece of material used to maintain a space between two things (_distanziatore_)|
|**Tap**|Short press on the touchscreen (_tocco_)|
|**Flick**|Quick directional swipe (_panoramica_)|
|**Swipe**|Fast movement across the screen (_velocissimo tocco in movimento_)|
|**Stylus**|Pen-like input tool for touchscreens|
|**Indium tin oxide**|Conductive material used in resistive screens|
|**Electromagnetic field**|Required by capacitive screens to register touch|

---

## Why Optical Is Winning

Optical-touch technology combines the best of both worlds:

- **Robust** (standard glass, no special treatment needed)
- **Bright** (no light-absorbing layers)
- **Cheap** to manufacture
- Supports **multi-touch** and works with **any input** including gloves

This makes it increasingly popular for both consumer and commercial devices.
