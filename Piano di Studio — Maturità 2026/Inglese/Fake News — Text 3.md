## What "Fake News" Is

**Collins English Dictionary — Word of the Year 2017:**

> "False, often sensational, information disseminated under the guise of news reporting."

**Three key traits:**

- **Looks real** — stories that appear to be genuine news
- **Deliberately false and misleading** — not accidental errors
- **Clickbait headlines** — designed to catch attention and encourage reading/sharing

**Widely shared on social media**, these stories fool people with potentially damaging **disinformation**.

---

## Why It Spreads So Easily

### 1. Technological ease

Copying, pasting, clicking and sharing content online is instant → helps these articles **proliferate**.

### 2. Emotional design

Articles are deliberately designed to **provoke an emotional response**, placed on specific sites to entice readers into sharing them widely.

### 3. Bots

"Fake news" articles may be generated and disseminated by **bots** — computer algorithms designed to **act like people sharing information**, but doing so quickly and automatically.

### 4. No source validation

People on social networking sites simply **don't take the time to validate the source** of a story. Many never make it past the headline before liking and sharing.

### Result

Fake news has become **one of the web's most pressing problems**, making it ever more difficult to distinguish between myth and reality.

### Platforms' response

Facebook, Twitter and Google have **promised to crack down** on fake news — but have had **very limited success** so far.

---

## Political Impact

Politics is often the focus of fake news, especially in the US:

- Pope Francis allegedly endorsed Donald Trump
- Barack Obama was said to have banned the Pledge of Allegiance in schools

→ Fake news **influences general elections** and public opinion.

---

## Fake News Sites

- Lots of fake news outlets exist and **numbers are growing**
- In 2017, **Google took action against 340 sites** that deceive people with their content
- They work because they **look like real news sources**

**Example:** _World News Daily Report_ (worldnewsdailyreport.com)

- Adopts a layout similar to many real news sites
- Articles virtually **indistinguishable in style** from real newspapers
- Some sites go further and **imitate legitimate outlets entirely**

## Who Creates Fake News?

|Type|Motivation|
|---|---|
|**Money-seekers**|Profit from clicks, regardless of content (e.g. Macedonian teenagers)|
|**Satirists**|Make a point, entertain, or both|
|**Political actors**|Influence elections and public opinion|

## Key Vocabulary

|Term|Meaning|
|---|---|
|**Fake news**|False information presented as real news|
|**Disinformation**|Deliberately false information|
|**Clickbait**|Link/headline designed to catch attention and encourage clicks|
|**Bot**|Computer algorithm designed to act like a human online|
|**Proliferate**|Spread or increase rapidly|
|**Disseminate**|Spread widely|
|**Validate**|Check the accuracy/truth of something|
|**Satirist**|Person who uses humour/irony to criticise or entertain|
